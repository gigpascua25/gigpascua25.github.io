Where a profitable retailer leaks margin

An analysis of 51,290 e-commerce sales lines that traces four years of flat margin to deep discounting.

Role
Team of six. I reported discounting as a major contributor to profit leakage, added the Ralph Lauren precedent, and presented the discount findings.
Tools
Spreadsheets, with AI-assisted insight generation from a structured prompt sheet.
Data
A global e-commerce retailer, 2014–2017: 51,290 sales lines, 25,728 orders, 1,970 returned orders, across 165 countries.

The question

Sales grew from about $2.26M in 2014 to $4.30M in 2017, but margin barely moved, from 11.0% to 11.7%. The business is profitable. We asked where profit is leaking, what appears to drive it, and what management can realistically do about it.

What we did

  1. Joined orders, returns and regional leads. We removed 63 duplicate return rows, rebuilt the broken Canada region join, and confirmed the totals still reconciled to the source to the cent.
  2. Tested five explanations for the low margin: discounting, product mix, geography, returns and customer segment.
  3. Measured margin by discount depth to find where sales start losing money.
  4. Turned the result into a staged recommendation with an illustrative scenario and a pilot.

What I found

I presented these two findings in the team's deck.

  • Discounts cost far more than returns. Return-related losses come to about $136,600, while sales discounted beyond 20% lost about $815,000, more than half of total profit. The data has no return reasons, so returns are a secondary opportunity.
  • Margin collapses with discount depth. Full-price sales keep about 25 cents of every dollar. Margin crosses zero at roughly a 20–30% discount and collapses past 30%. Losses begin in the 21–30% band, which is why the gate is set at 30%: everyday promotions stay friction-free and the severe losses stop.

The team ruled out the other explanations. All 17 sub-categories earn 21–32% at full price, and the three customer segments all sit between 11.5% and 12%.

What we recommended

  • Put a gate on discounts above 30%, with approval for exceptions.
  • Pilot it for 90 days in Western Asia, which has real sales volume and a negative margin.
  • Roll it out company-wide only if profit improves without an unacceptable fall in volume.

As precedent, Ralph Lauren deliberately cut markdowns from 2017 and grew margin without losing comparable sales. It is a premium brand and price sensitivity may differ, which is another reason to pilot first.

Limits we flagged

  • Discounts were not randomised, so every finding is an association, not a cause.
  • There is no cost-of-goods or currency column. We lead with percentages and treat dollar totals as close estimates.
  • Shipping sits outside the profit figure, a level shift that affects every line equally.
